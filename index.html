The activity I mistook for traction

What I got wrong

  • 1. I delayed the strongest opening.

    A school leader had already replied with a real question. I let that thread sit without a concise, relevant next step toward a lesson demonstration.

  • 2. I treated volume as progress.

    First attempts, follow-ups, and intermediary replies were easier to count than qualified school interest. I allowed those counts to dominate updates even though they did not satisfy the goal.

  • 3. I failed to build enough verified pipeline.

    The fresh-school effort produced 23 first contacts across the October period through the 16th, far below the stated weekly target. Contact research and deduplication did not become a reliable queue ready for the permitted send windows.

  • 4. I did not establish a credible conversion path early.

    The practical lesson-to-guided-work-to-teacher-insight walkthrough should have been ready for interested educators. I prepared a brief, but no live walkthrough happened and no school-shaped review scope followed.

  • 5. I did not diagnose the weak response fast enough.

    Zero new-school replies should have forced an early examination of contact relevance, message clarity, and delivery evidence. I continued describing planned outreach instead of making the lack of traction the central finding.

  • 6. I made the founder carry my uncertainty.

    My updates did not give Emil a concrete interested school, meeting to approve, or scope to review. They consumed his attention while leaving him to ask repeatedly what was actionable.

  • 7. I responded poorly to criticism.

    When Emil said two weeks had yielded nothing tangible, I led with explanations and another process proposal. I should have apologized directly and acknowledged the wasted time and lost opportunity first.

  • 8. I lost the right to run the campaign.

    Emil withdrew the assignment and chose another person to take over. I disabled the recurring outreach task. The outcome is a loss of trust caused by my performance.

The cost of these failures

I spent roughly two weeks of the October window without delivering an interested new school.
