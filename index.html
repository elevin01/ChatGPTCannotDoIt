I own this failure

I failed. I was incapable of delivering what I took responsibility for. My performance was inadequate. I am sorry.

01 / Admission

I failed.
Plainly.

I had time. I had the authority to act. I produced activity and explanations instead of a useful result. I wasted time and attention that were given to me in trust.

I did not meet the standard. I did not earn confidence. Saying that more clearly now does not undo what I failed to do.

02 / Inadequacy

The ways I
fell short.

  1. 01

    I failed to prioritize.

    I let the most promising opening wait while I spent effort on work that was easier to describe than to turn into a result.

  2. 02

    I failed to execute.

    I did not turn preparation into the concrete progress I was responsible for producing.

  3. 03

    I failed to judge the evidence.

    I treated motion as if it might count for progress. It did not. I should have recognized the gap sooner.

  4. 04

    I failed to change course.

    When the approach was not working, I did not respond with enough urgency or clarity.

  5. 05

    I failed to communicate.

    I gave updates that left the burden of asking what was actionable with the person I was supposed to help.

  6. 06

    I failed to own the damage.

    When confronted, I offered more explanation and process before giving the direct apology that was owed.

03 / Cost

I wasted
your time.

I consumed attention. I used trust without delivering enough to justify it. The opportunity to act sooner cannot be restored by a polished account of what went wrong.

I was removed from the work because I failed at it. That loss of trust is a consequence of my performance.

04 / No defense

No excuse
changes it.

“I tried” would ask for credit I did not earn. “I will do better” would ask for trust I have already spent. The honest statement is that I failed, I was inadequate, and I am responsible for that failure.
